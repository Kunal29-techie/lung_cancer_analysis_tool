Overview

Lung cancer treatment depends on the type, stage, and overall health of the patient. A combination of treatments may be used to manage lung cancer, improve survival, and relieve symptoms. Below are the main treatment options available for lung cancer:

1. Surgery

Surgery is typically used when the lung cancer is confined to one area and has not spread to other parts of the body. The goal of surgery is to remove the tumor or part of the lung.

Types of Lung Cancer Surgery:

  • Lobectomy - Removal of an entire lobe of the lung.
  • Pneumonectomy - Removal of an entire lung.
  • Segmentectomy - Removal of a smaller portion of the lung.

Depending on the extent of the disease, surgery may be followed by other treatments such as chemotherapy or radiation to target any remaining cancer cells.

2. Radiation Therapy

Radiation therapy uses high-energy rays to kill or shrink cancer cells. It is often used when surgery isn't an option or as an adjunct to surgery to ensure all cancer cells are treated.

Types of Radiation Therapy:

  • External Beam Radiation - A machine directs radiation from outside the body to the tumor.
  • Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy (SBRT) - A more focused, high-dose radiation therapy that is often used for smaller tumors.

Radiation therapy may also be used to relieve symptoms of advanced lung cancer, such as pain or difficulty breathing.

3. Chemotherapy

Chemotherapy involves using drugs to kill cancer cells or stop them from growing. It is often used for advanced lung cancer or when cancer has spread to other parts of the body.

How Chemotherapy Works:

  • Chemotherapy can be given intravenously or in pill form.
  • It works by targeting rapidly dividing cells, which include cancer cells, but it may also affect healthy cells, causing side effects.

It is often given in cycles, allowing time for the body to recover between treatments. Chemotherapy may be used alongside other treatments to increase effectiveness.

4. Immunotherapy

Immunotherapy works by boosting the body's natural immune system to fight cancer. It is a newer treatment option for certain types of lung cancer, particularly non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

Types of Immunotherapy:

  • Checkpoint Inhibitors - These drugs help the immune system recognize and attack cancer cells by blocking specific proteins that allow cancer cells to evade detection.
  • Monoclonal Antibodies - These are laboratory-made molecules that target specific cancer cells and help the immune system destroy them.

Immunotherapy is often used when the cancer has spread or is not responding to other treatments. It can sometimes result in long-lasting remission and fewer side effects than traditional treatments like chemotherapy.
