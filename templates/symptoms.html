Overview

Lung cancer may not show symptoms in its early stages, which is why it is important to be aware of potential signs. If you notice any persistent symptoms, consult with a healthcare provider for evaluation and diagnosis.

1. Common Symptoms

Early symptoms of lung cancer can resemble those of other respiratory conditions, but it's important to take notice if they persist or worsen:

  • Persistent Cough - A cough that doesn't go away or worsens over time.
  • Coughing Up Blood - Even small amounts of blood in your sputum or phlegm should be checked.
  • Chest Pain - Pain in the chest that is persistent or worsens with deep breaths or coughing.
  • Shortness of Breath - Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath, even with minimal activity.
  • Wheezing - A high-pitched whistling sound when breathing, especially with exhalation.

If you experience any of these symptoms, it is important to see a doctor for an evaluation.

2. Advanced Symptoms

As the cancer progresses, more severe symptoms may develop. These symptoms may indicate the cancer has spread to other parts of the body:

  • Unexplained Weight Loss - Losing weight without trying or for no clear reason.
  • Fatigue - Persistent tiredness or feeling weak, even after rest.
  • Bone Pain - Pain in the bones, especially in the back, hips, or shoulders, which could indicate cancer spread to the bones.
  • Swelling in the Neck or Face - Swelling in the neck or face due to pressure from a growing tumor.
  • Neurological Symptoms - Symptoms such as headaches, blurred vision, or weakness in the limbs, which may occur if cancer has spread to the brain.

If you experience any of these advanced symptoms, seek medical attention immediately. Early detection of lung cancer increases the chances of successful treatment.
